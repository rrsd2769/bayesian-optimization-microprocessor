%=============================================================
% CONCLUSION
%=============================================================

Treating a convolutional network's architecture and the scheduler that
runs it as one six-dimensional design problem, instead of tuning each
alone, let a directed search come close to a reference front enumerated
over $92{,}412$ designs at a small fraction of the cost. It matched the
single-objective energy optimum in 30 evaluations where grid search used
972, and it recovered $98.4\%$ of the reference hypervolume in 100. Its
margin over random sampling at equal cost held on every repeated seed,
30 of 30 and 15 of 15, so the advantage belongs to the search method and
not to one lucky run.

Two decisions kept the search cheap to run. The four quantities come
from two kinds of model: closed-form or regression models for accuracy,
memory, and energy, and a discrete-event simulation for latency alone.
An evaluation took milliseconds, yet the queueing that latency depends
on was simulated and not approximated away. Constraints also act inside
the acquisition function, which steers proposals toward designs likely
to be acceptable and spends few evaluations on designs no accuracy gain
could rescue. Neither decision depends on this network or this device.
Any joint architecture--scheduler problem trades evaluation realism
against evaluation count, and pricing each quantity by how hard it is to
obtain keeps a directed search affordable without a coarser model of the
target hardware.

The reference front rests on a fixed bit-width grid, three simulated time
slices, and a single simulated core, and the accuracy behind every result
is a regression estimate, not a measurement at each candidate. The next
step is to test whether the evaluation-cost advantage survives as each of
these simplifications is removed in turn: a finer bit-width grid with real
4-bit kernels, a heterogeneous multi-core target, and accuracy measured
on quantized hardware. Training one network that specializes to many
deployment targets~\cite{cai2022enable} could replace the regression
surface. Running the selected designs on a production tinyML runtime with
measured power~\cite{lin2023tinyml,ray2022tinyml} would then close the
remaining gap between simulated and measured performance.
