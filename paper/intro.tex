% INTRODUCTION
%=============================================================

A microcontroller that runs a convolutional network usually has no fan,
draws on a coin cell or a harvested supply, and shares its single core
with the other tasks of a real-time operating system
(RTOS)~\cite{tsoukas2024tinyml,mazumder2021microai,ray2022tinyml}. Energy
per inference limits such a device more than anything else, and two
groups of decisions set it. Depth, channel width, and weight precision
fix how many multiply--accumulate (MAC) operations the network needs and how much
memory it occupies. The time slice, the scheduling policy, and the
clock-scaling factor then fix how quickly, and at what power, the RTOS
carries those operations out. The groups interact. A lower clock saves
power but stretches every inference, and a smaller network frees latency
headroom that a lower clock can then spend.

Tool flows usually tune one group while holding the other fixed. Neural
architecture search~\cite{elsken2019survey}, in its hardware-aware form,
co-explores network topology against a given accelerator, compiler, or
device~\cite{chitty2023hwnas,jiang2020coexploration,sekanina2021survey,chen2023chanas}.
MCUNet, built for microcontrollers, co-designs the network with its
inference library but leaves the RTOS scheduler and the core clock out of
the search~\cite{lin2023tinyml}. On the
other side, energy-aware real-time scheduling is a mature area of its
own~\cite{bambagini2016energy,sheikh2018multicore}, recently extended to
learned dynamic voltage and frequency scaling (DVFS) for inference
workloads~\cite{zhang2024dvfo}; there, the network is an input to the
problem and is never searched.

Bayesian optimization (BO) fits problems where each evaluation is costly
and no gradient is available~\cite{shahriari2016review}. It has been used
for multi-objective hyperparameter search in neural-network accelerator
design~\cite{parsa2020bayesian}. On benchmark problems, multi-objective
architecture search can now be scored against known
fronts~\cite{lu2024nasmoo},
with evolutionary algorithms as the more common
search method~\cite{deb2002nsga2,lyu2024surrogate,ma2024paretowise}, and
regression models of deployment energy and latency cut down how often a
candidate must be profiled on hardware~\cite{mazumder2024regtune}. In
none of these studies do RTOS scheduler parameters share a search space
with the architecture.

Here they do. Network architecture and scheduler configuration form one
six-dimensional problem, searched with BO and with neither half fixed in
advance. The same design space carries two objectives: minimum energy
under accuracy and latency constraints, and the accuracy--energy
trade-off under the latency constraint alone. Both are scored against a
reference built by enumerating $92{,}412$ designs. Single-objective BO
reaches the enumerated energy optimum in 30 evaluations, where grid
search spends 972. Multi-objective BO recovers $98.4\%$ of the reference
hypervolume in 100 evaluations. Random sampling at equal cost trails on
both problems and on every repeated seed, 30 of 30 for the first and 15
of 15 for the second.
