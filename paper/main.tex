\begin{abstract}
The energy a microcontroller spends per inference depends on the
network, through depth, width, and weight precision, and on the real-time
operating system (RTOS) that schedules it, through time slice, priority, and
clock. This study searches both as one six-variable problem, accuracy
against energy per inference under accuracy, latency, deadline-miss, and
memory constraints. Accuracy is a table of $26$ trainings with
whole-network quantization at $4$, $8$, $16$, and $32$ bits. Cycles and
memory are calibrated on an STM32 board, and on eight further networks the
cycle predictions err by $4.8\%$ on average and $7.6\%$ at most. Latency and
deadline misses come from a deterministic RTOS simulation, and energy from
datasheet currents. Full enumeration of $12{,}000$ designs gives a reference
front of four designs among $378$ acceptable ones. Six methods are compared
over $20$ seeds at $100$ evaluations. Joint qNEHVI reaches a median of $100\%$
of the reference hypervolume, and $90\%$ in $28$ evaluations; qNParEGO reaches
$99.6\%$, MOTPE $91.0\%$, Random $74.8\%$, and NSGA-II $56.0\%$. A two-stage
search that fixes the scheduler first stalls at $91.2\%$.
\end{abstract}
